# Hierarchical-Data-Analysis-Using-Bayesian-Nonparametric-Models

Bayesian nonparametric models based on the Dirichlet Process provide a flexible framework for modeling data with latent clustering structures of unknown cardinality. However, their extension to hierarchical settings through the Hierarchical Dirichlet Process entails substantial limitations in scenarios involving multiple levels of grouping and a large number of groups per level; furthermore, it imposes the sharing of atoms across levels, an assumption that is difficult to justify in many contexts. This work proposes two alternative Bayesian nonparametric formulations for hierarchical data, both featuring a hierarchical structure in the variance component: a model with a single Dirichlet Process on the location component (M2), and a model with three independent Dirichlet Processes on the location component, in correspondence with the levels of the data hierarchy (M3). Additionally, a parametric hierarchical Bayesian model (M1) is developed as a benchmark to quantify the contribution of the Bayesian nonparametric formulations. The proposed models were validated through an experimental design comprising multiple simulation scenarios, which provided empirical evidence of the dominance of Model M3 over Model M2 when the data exhibit a genuinely hierarchical structure. Subsequently, the three models were applied to a case study of the Saber 11 test scores in Colombia, a setting characterized by a natural three-level hierarchical structure (students, municipalities, and departments) and by marked socioeconomic and territorial heterogeneity. The empirical application reveals a latent clustering structure that justifies the use of Bayesian nonparametric models: Model M2, which proves to be the most suitable formulation for this case owing to its balance between predictive quality, interpretability, and computational feasibility, identifies a structure of seven non-trivial latent profiles in the determinants of academic achievement. This work thus contributes both to the methodological development of Bayesian nonparametric models for complex hierarchical data and to the empirical analysis of the determinants of academic achievement in Colombian secondary education.
